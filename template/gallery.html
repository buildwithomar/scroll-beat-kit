One item, every move.

Scroll. A window with chips around it performs each move the skill has an example of: flip, spin, split, fold, backflip, go responsive, come apart in 3D. The text says what each move could mean in a story. A real deck picks four and makes them say something; these are a starting vocabulary, not the limit of it.

Move 0 · assembleAn item, with chips around it.

The draft resolves and the chips dock on its edges. The item can be any product, plan, page or offer; the chips are its features, integrations or proof.

Move 1 · flipFlip it to the other side.

rotateY to 180° reveals a back face. Use it for before and after, problem and fix, or the customer's view and yours.

Move 2 · spinSpin it a turn and a half.

Degrees just add up, so 540° is simply a longer move. Use it for a reveal, "from every angle", or a change of state.

Move 3 · split ×2Split it into two.

B enters behind A, then they part. A/B variants, old and new, you and them.

Move 4 · split ×3Then into three.

A third joins the row: pricing tiers, three use cases, good, better, best. On a narrow stage the row becomes a fanned hand.

Move 5 · foldFold back into one.

The copies slide home behind the original, and the chips return. Many tools become one platform; the options collapse into the answer.

Move 6 · backflipDo a backflip.

rotateX through 360°. The camera budgets the extra height a flip paints under perspective. Use it sparingly; it's a celebration.

Move 7 · responsiveMake it responsive.

The window narrows into a phone and its content reflows to fit, not cropped but re-laid out. Use it for "works on every device", desktop to mobile, or page to app.

Move 8 · 3D partsTake it apart in 3D.

The item tilts isometric and its layers lift apart in depth. Use it for what's under the hood, how it's built, or the stack behind the screen.
